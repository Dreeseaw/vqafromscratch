**Run-by-Run**
Run 1 recovered the missing final eval for the Cement+GQA bridge experiment and added a `5k` exact-match GQA check. The bridge itself is not bad in absolute terms, but its VQAv2 score is clearly worse than the plain Cement champion. The GQA eval in [run1_gqa_5k.json](/home/wdree/percy/vqafromscratch/logs/mmovernight_batch_v1_20260327_000456/run1_gqa_5k.json) was `0.7092` overall, with `exist` strongest at `0.7854`. I would treat the `count=0.5000` bucket as low-confidence because that slice is tiny in this sampled evaluation.

Run 2 tested the key hypothesis that a GQA-shaped perceiver might export better compressible evidence. It did not. The compressed result in [run2_best_full.json](/home/wdree/percy/vqafromscratch/logs/mmovernight_batch_v1_20260327_000456/run2_best_full.json) regressed across every major category relative to the best SigLIP-only compressed line, and its probe in [run2_probe.json](/home/wdree/percy/vqafromscratch/logs/mmovernight_batch_v1_20260327_000456/run2_probe.json) was also weak at `0.4688` overall. That points to worse tokens, not just worse LM consumption.

Run 3 was cleaner. Adding a small amount of GQA during compression on top of the already-strong dual-VM perceiver did not create a large gain, but it did avoid the degradation seen in the heavier `GQA + pointing + L_ground` run. The result is close enough to the dual-VM `K=8` baseline that I would read it as “GQA at compression is mildly compatible, grounding was the drag” rather than as a real new frontier.

Run 4 answers the token-budget question pretty directly: `K=12` does not buy back OCR. Overall VQAv2 stayed basically flat, the OCR subset stayed poor, and the probe in [run4_probe.json](/home/wdree/percy/vqafromscratch/logs/mmovernight_batch_v1_20260327_000456/run4_probe.json) dropped sharply to `0.4071`. That is strong evidence that simply exporting more tokens is not enough; the bottleneck still collapses or ignores the OCR-bearing features.

Run 5 combined the two weaker ideas and stayed weak. Both the full eval and the probe in [run5_probe.json](/home/wdree/percy/vqafromscratch/logs/mmovernight_batch_v1_20260327_000456/run5_probe.json) were poor relative to the better compressed lines. So there is no evidence here that “GQA bridge + more tokens” is the right recovery path.

**Probe Read**
Among the new runs, the strongest probe was Run 3 at `0.5046` in [run3_probe.json](/home/wdree/percy/vqafromscratch/logs/mmovernight_batch_v1_20260327_000456/run3_probe.json). That is still below the prior dual-VM `K=8` probe from [65_dualvm_compression_report_2026-03-26.md](/home/wdree/percy/vqafromscratch/tasks/mm_bridge/docs/65_dualvm_compression_report_2026-03-26.md), but it is far healthier than the `K=12` probe or the GQA-bridge probes. The probe pattern matches the eval story: the only line that plausibly preserved dense, useful compressed semantics was dual-VM `K=8` with a light GQA mix at compression.

**Bottom Line**
The overnight batch did not produce a new frontier. The best actionable conclusion is narrower: if GQA is used at all in this regime, it looks safer as a light compression-stage regularizer on top of the strong dual-VM perceiver than as a bridge-stage training ingredient. The stronger negative conclusion is that the OCR problem is not just “`K=8` is too small.” `K=12` failed to recover OCR, and the ViTSTR stream still receives very little effective routing. The next serious OCR-preservation attempt should focus on explicit OCR-aware retention or routing pressure, not just more bottleneck tokens or more GQA mixed into the wrong stage.
